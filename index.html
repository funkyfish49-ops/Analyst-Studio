        ❓ How TopicCast Studio works

        Pick a style, enter a topic, and a research pipeline of AI agents produces a fully narrated, source-cited audio episode.

        Phone & desktop layout

        The app auto-detects whether it's running on a phone or a desktop and switches its layout — the Monitor watchlist is a dropdown on phones and a clickable side panel on desktops. Override it any time in Settings → Layout (Auto / Phone / Desktop).

        Podcast styles

        • 🎙️ Story (default) — the topic's journey: how it started, where it stands today, and what's next. Two hosts, conversational.
        • 📜 History — the full history and evolution of a topic, traced from its origins era by era through to today's context.
        • ⚖️ Debate — a moderated, evidence-based debate. Pro and Con advocates research their strongest case, an Evidence Referee fact-checks every claim and calls a winner, and the MODERATOR announces the verdict in the episode. The verdict always follows the verified facts.
        • 📰 News — today's headlines on a subject, verified for recency and accuracy, read as a broadcast newsread by a single ANCHOR voice.

        The Analysts tab

        The 📈 Analysts tab runs the Trading Analyst Pipeline for today's date: the Market Scanner pulls the day's analyst calls, the CFA Analyst Desk turns them into trade recommendations (market-native action verbs, ENTER / NO ENTRY decisions, confidence, position size and risk/reward in dollars on a $100k book), the Debating Researchers argue every idea for and against, and the Investment Manager makes her trade choice. The board shows her choice and her point-form Research Report — concise bullets of statistical indicators and precise qualitative statements, with key figures bolded and important dates and trading terms highlighted — press Hear her take to hear it spoken in her voice — tap it again to pause the take (the badge flips to a pause button while it plays), and once more to resume where it left off — plus a tile per CFA recommendation; pressing a tile loads that idea's for/against arguments. The Opportunities side panel files every daily call under its date — the loaded call stays expanded for working the board, and past calls (synced while signed in) expand by date, then by category, to their opportunities. At the top of the tab there are two ways to run the desk: Analysts Call produces today's full call — its scout leads with the forward 10-day catalyst calendar (earnings, auctions, CPI/Fed prints, contracts resolving and sports inside the window, every date verified three ways: in-window, weekday-matched and source-confirmed) and treats today's street research as the secondary source, and any idea that fails price validation is dropped from the board before the show is written — and next to it the 📋 Ledger button copies log-ready entries for the whole board in the daily scan's exact ledger format (one block per idea: analyst call, entry decision, confidence, edge, size, risk/reward, 10-day horizon, status pending) for pasting into the append-only opportunities log. The call can also run itself: under Daily auto-call in the Settings tab, pick Prompt (a banner offers today's call when you open the app) or Auto (today's call runs on open — prices are verified at run time, so the desk works when you open it; one auto attempt per day). Next to it the Analyze form runs the same research machinery on a single opportunity of your choosing — type the opportunity (e.g. NVDA into earnings), pick its scan category from the dropdown, and use the 🌍 Context button next to it to select the country the symbol trades in — the desk then researches, argues and judges the idea in that country's market context: native exchange and currency, regulator and central bank, national data sources and macro drivers. Before the desk runs, the submitted symbol is also resolved against a live market-data feed (Yahoo Finance quotes through a public reader proxy — international exchange suffixes like .AX, .TO, .L, .T supported): the resolved live quote is handed to the desk as the verified price, and an UNCONFIRMED price on the final call is replaced with the live quote (an unresolvable symbol simply skips the quote — the run is unaffected). On Auto it infers the country from the exchange suffix (.TO, .L, .T…) or market keywords (TSX, FTSE, Nikkei…), defaulting to the United States; press Analyze, and the CFA desk researches it, the Debating Researchers argue it for and against, the Investment Manager delivers her verdict (with the same price validation as the daily call), and the result is displayed in the same call-panel format — execution strip, choice badge, research report, expert verdict and chart. While the desk is working, the Analyze button shows a spinner; if the run fails it turns into an exclamation mark — press it to retry. Every call panel opens with that idea's exact trade execution details at the top — entry decision, position size, risk/reward (target, stop, max loss, reward:risk) and confidence. Prices are validated end to end: every idea must carry a freshly verified as-of price (the desk is forbidden from quoting prices from analyst notes, price targets or older research as current), the manager re-verifies each price before deciding, and a built-in price check flags any idea that ships without a verified quote, whose as-of price is dated, or whose target or stop sits on the wrong side of the live price — flagged prices show an amber warning in the call panel and the production log. The Showrunner then turns the meeting into a playable episode, with the MANAGER speaking in her own distinct voice. When your Experts roster holds an expert whose domain fits the day's board, an interactive guest joins the show: the pipeline picks the best-matched roster expert, and in the show's outlook the EXPERT weighs the manager's call in first person — stating their position (agree, disagree, or agree with reservations) with dated, proven reasons drawn from their recorded knowledge and fresh searches, pushing back on the weakest link, and putting one sharp question to the desk, which the manager answers live. The Analysts board shows their verdict as a point right beside her call — AGREE, DISAGREE or AGREE WITH RESERVATIONS, with their stated reason. The guest speaks in their own distinct voice — pick it under Expert guest voice in Settings (Studio voices), or it auto-pairs; with browser voices it gets its own pitch. The Investment Manager keeps asking the CFA desk for more ideas until her book holds 5 recommendations at 80%+ confidence in each of the four daily-scan categories — STOCKS, PREDICTIONS, BONDS, OTHER — so expect several desk rounds before the show is written. Her re-asks are capped by a desk clock, a time limit you can configure in the Settings tab (default 5 minutes): when the clock runs out she produces the show with whatever high-conviction ideas the desk has delivered so far, and the log keeps her running tally per category. Stock ideas show a live daily candle chart (with technicals) sourced from Finviz beside the desk write-up — press a tile to see it, and pressing the chart opens its live data-source page in a new tab (the Finviz quote page for stocks, the FRED series page for bond yields). Every category has its own contextual data panel: bond ideas map to the matching yield series on FRED (the St. Louis Fed) — treasuries, TIPS real yields, high-yield credit spreads; prediction-market ideas link to the live contract on Polymarket and Kalshi; sports and odds ideas link to current lines across books. If a chart source fails or is blocked, the panel tries its backup source (Finviz's new chart CDN, then its legacy endpoint; FRED with a rolling 1-year window, then the default range) and then links straight to the live chart — Finviz and Yahoo Finance for stocks, FRED for yields — so a failed image never leaves a blank panel. In the Episode tab, the daily call displays as a live indicator board instead of the raw script: one minimal card per idea, grouped by the four categories, showing the quantitative indicators (entry decision, position size, risk/reward, confidence) and the qualitative ones (the desk's case, the researchers' best for and against arguments). As the manager reads the script and discusses each opportunity, that idea's card lights up and the indicators she is talking about highlight; toggle back to the raw script with the Show script button — the script is always saved with the episode. From research to verdict, the desk agents apply an advanced analytics toolkit: separating prediction from causation, Double/Debiased-ML-style confounder reasoning, causal-forest thinking about which sub-groups an effect concentrates in, agent-based simulation of emergent system behavior (bank runs, market failures, supply-chain disruptions beyond DSGE-style equilibrium), market-design analysis for pricing claims, and data-harmonization checks on messy or digitized series.

        The Monitor tab

        The 📊 Monitor tab tracks everything your latest episode claimed. Pick an entry from the watchlist dropdown — the trading opportunities from the analyst board (grouped into their trading categories: STOCKS / PREDICTIONS / BONDS / OTHER) alongside Topics, one entry per episode segment listed by its whole topic name; re-checked entries carry a status marker (✓ / ↻ / ✖). The right panel shows, top to bottom: the verdict or point (the call with its action badge and thesis tag, or the segment's point), the quantitative proof (entry, size, risk/reward, confidence — or the dated sources behind a topic), the qualitative proof (the case, for/against arguments, or the segment script), and a still-valid check. Press Re-check with live search and the Monitor Analyst agent searches the live web for what has changed since the episode was produced, then rules the item STILL VALID, SHOULD CHANGE, or EVOLVED — with a severity grade on the change impact: HIGH means the direction flipped (a buy is now a sell — requires immediate attention), MEDIUM means the call stands but entry, size or timing must change, LOW means minor nuance. The Monitor Analyst weighs the evidence with the same advanced analytics toolkit as the desk — causal vs. predictive claims, heterogeneous effects, emergent-behavior simulation, and data-quality checks — before ruling. Re-checks are saved with the episode, and any sources the Monitor Analyst finds are added to the Sources & Dates registry. Producing a new episode starts a fresh watchlist.

        Cloud sync & track record

        The 🏁 Results tab is the ledger of every opportunity the desk has ever produced — one line per idea, with a category filter, status filter, free-text filter and sort at the top, and a running summary of won / lost / void / expired / open with closed and open P&L. Each line shows the entry price, stop, target, the current price and the position's profit / loss; press a line for the details behind it (decision, size, max loss, confidence, entropic F, the case, and how it closed). Positions are tracked the way their category works: stock and bond positions close themselves when the target or stop is crossed, prediction-market positions when the contract resolves (price ≈ 100¢ / 0¢), and odds & sports positions are resolved with the Won / Lost buttons (odds have no universal price scale). Anything still open past its 10-day horizon completes as expired. Feed prices with the per-line ✎ Price (type the quote) or 🔎 Live (the desk agent searches the current price) — the same 📈 Position panel lives in the Monitor tab. Signed in, the ledger reads and writes your cloud track record directly; signed out it shows this device's current episode.

        The Settings tab has a ☁️ Cloud sync card: sign in with Google and every episode you produce — podcast or analyst call — is saved to your Firebase cloud library, together with one trackable document per board opportunity and per episode topic. The Monitor tab doubles as your track record: each re-check appends to that item's cloud history, and the 🏆 Track record row lets you resolve any opportunity as Won, Lost or Void — or reopen it — with the outcome saved with the episode and synced to the cloud. Your ☁️ Cloud sync panel in Settings lists every past episode automatically, and your devices open the latest one by themselves — tap any episode in the library to bring it onto this device with its full watchlist and track record intact.         🧠 The Knowledge tab

        The 🧠 Knowledge tab is the desk's living knowledge base. Every card is one established fact or theme with claims (each with a 0-100 confidence and an optional basis), dated sources, link keys, and a place in a category facet rubric — six evolving categories (Market, Economic, Industry, Social media, Stock-specific, Other), each with its own rubric of facets that make depth measurable. Cards are versioned: every commit, edit, verification or retirement lands in a per-card version timeline. Freshness is policy-driven — each card declares its own re-verify window, and cards past it go stale, then overdue, surfacing in the 🧐 Review queue where you can mark them verified, revise or retire them in one click. The 🗺️ Coverage & depth map shows how many cards cover each facet per category (with their average claim confidence), a bar per category, and how far each lens is trusted: once a category accumulates 5 resolved outcomes, its learned reliability weights (long / short) start showing beside it — below that it is still learning. Use ＋ New card to add knowledge manually, or expand a card and press ✎ Edit — the editor takes claims one per line in the form text | confidence | basis and sources as url | date. The desk is wired to this base: while forming calls the desk and manager read the established cards and the earned lens-reliability weights (a lens activates at 5 resolved outcomes), the manager records which lenses moved each call in the CALL DATA, those vectors are frozen into decisions at board freeze, and every Won / Lost resolution feeds the lens weights automatically — voids, expiries and re-resolutions never double-count. The 📡 Sources & digestion card feeds the base: add any URL as a one-time read or a repeatable feed, press 🍽️ Digest now (or 🍽️ Digest due for every pending source), and the research agent reads the source and proposes new cards, claims to add to existing cards, and contradictions — the proposals wait in the review queue, where you save, apply or edit them one by one. Cards, decisions, weights and sources sync through your private Firebase cloud: while you are signed in the whole base syncs itself between phone and PC — every write pushes a few seconds later, and remote changes pull in when you sign in, when you return to the tab and every few minutes. There are no sync buttons; nothing to press. Everything also stays stored locally on this device. The 📥 Import cards button in the Coverage card loads starter packs that ship with the app (the Trading Economics pack catalogues its market and macro data sections) or pasted card JSON — every entry is validated against the rubric, invalid entries are skipped, and cards whose category + title already exists are left alone. The 📈 Evolution card on the Knowledge tab plots each lens's weight history and the P&L of the decisions it shaped, with aligned-versus-dissenting win rates showing whether a lens's trust is earned.

        🌀 Entropic second opinion

        Every BUY / SELL opportunity the desk produces is auto-scored by an embedded causal-entropic engine (F = T·∇Sτ, after Alex Wissner-Gross's work on intelligence as freedom-seeking): it simulates thousands of ten-day futures from the idea's own board numbers — conviction, reward:risk, position size — and asks whether committing to the trade preserves more of the desk's future freedom of action than staying in cash. The board tiles, indicator cards and Monitor panels show the resulting 🌀 F chip (green = the trade keeps more future options open, red = it forecloses them), and every opportunity carries a 🌀 Entropic deep-dive — its regime priors, expected value, ∇Sτ and impairment probability, with editable priors you can re-score against your own view. PREDICTIONS and OTHER ideas are proxy-scored as price paths, so treat those F values as rougher. Treat the score as a second opinion beside the desk's expected value, never a replacement for analyst judgment: walk-forward backtests of this engine show entropic scores can be anti-predictive on some structures — which is exactly why the app surfaces the raw F instead of hiding it. When cloud sync is on, each opportunity's entropic score is stored in your cloud track record alongside its outcome, so over time you can see whether high-F calls really did keep more options open.

The dashboard loads when you open the tab, reloads on ⟳ Refresh indicators, and keeps its last successful read cached for offline use — the keyless BLS public access is capped at about 25 dashboard loads per day, so the refresh button is not unlimited. Every value links to its source: press an indicator's label to open the underlying series page (Treasury or FRED for yields, BLS for labour and prices, World Bank for global growth). Press ▶ Analysts Call and a business journalist researches the latest business and economic headlines via live web search (needs your Mistral API key) and delivers a spoken one-minute update — press the button again to pause it, and once more to resume — while each headline appears beside the update as a named link that opens the source website in a new tab. The update speaks through your configured voice engine (browser voices or Studio voices), and the last one stays on the tab for replay with Hear again.         3 · Audio & voices

        Pick a voice engine in the Episode tab:

        • 🎙️ Studio voices (recommended) — natural, human-quality speech generated by Mistral Voxtral with your API key. Choose the voice model: Voxtral TTS 3 (voxtral-mini-tts-3-0) is the latest and most natural generation; Voxtral TTS 2 (voxtral-mini-tts-2603) is the previous one. With Voxtral 3 you can also set expressiveness (1.5 calm → 4.5 animated) to tune how lively the delivery sounds. Debate episodes get a third voice slot for the CON debater (auto-picked as another distinct studio voice); in browser mode the CON debater uses the host voice pitched deeper, so all three debaters sound different.
        • 🌐 Browser voices — instant and offline, using your system's speech synthesis. The app prefers the smoothest neural/remote voices available, expands symbols and abbreviations for natural reading, and auto-pairs a male and a female voice for the two hosts (dropdowns are gender-labelled; pitch is subtly nudged when only same-gender voices exist).

        Follow along as each spoken line is highlighted; estimated runtime assumes ~155 words per minute.

        Pacing & natural pauses

        The narrator delivers the script sentence by sentence, with real breathing room: full stops get ~0.4 s, questions and exclamations land ~0.5 s, ellipses hold ~0.7 s, and speaker turns pause ~0.8 s so hosts don't talk over each other. A standalone “…” beat line (shown as “· · ·” in the script) becomes a ~1 s dramatic pause after a big point, and every section break rests ~1.5 s before the story continues. Em-dashes become mid-sentence beats, so points land the way a human host would make them.
