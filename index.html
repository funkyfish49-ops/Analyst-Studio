        ❓ How TopicCast Studio works

        Pick a style, enter a topic, and a research pipeline of AI agents produces a fully narrated, source-cited audio episode.

        Phone & desktop layout

        The app auto-detects whether it's running on a phone or a desktop and switches its layout — the Monitor watchlist is a dropdown on phones and a clickable side panel on desktops. Override it any time in Settings → Layout (Auto / Phone / Desktop).

        Podcast styles

        • 🎙️ Story (default) — the topic's journey: how it started, where it stands today, and what's next. Two hosts, conversational.
        • 📜 History — the full history and evolution of a topic, traced from its origins era by era through to today's context.
        • ⚖️ Debate — a moderated, evidence-based debate. Pro and Con advocates research their strongest case, an Evidence Referee fact-checks every claim and calls a winner, and the MODERATOR announces the verdict in the episode. The verdict always follows the verified facts.
        • 📰 News — today's headlines on a subject, verified for recency and accuracy, read as a broadcast newsread by a single ANCHOR voice.

        The Analysts tab

        The 📈 Analysts tab runs the Trading Analyst Pipeline for today's date: the Market Scanner pulls the day's analyst calls, the CFA Analyst Desk turns them into trade recommendations (market-native action verbs, ENTER / NO ENTRY decisions, confidence, position size and risk/reward in dollars on a $100k book), the Debating Researchers argue every idea for and against, and the Investment Manager makes her trade choice. The board shows her choice and her point-form Research Report — concise bullets of statistical indicators and precise qualitative statements, with key figures bolded and important dates and trading terms highlighted — press Hear her take to hear it spoken in her voice — tap it again to pause the take (the badge flips to a pause button while it plays), and once more to resume where it left off — plus a tile per CFA recommendation; pressing a tile loads that idea's for/against arguments. At the top of the tab there are two ways to run the desk: Analysts Call produces today's full call, and next to it the Analyze form runs the same research machinery on a single opportunity of your choosing — type the opportunity (e.g. NVDA into earnings), pick its scan category from the dropdown, press Analyze, and the CFA desk researches it, the Debating Researchers argue it for and against, the Investment Manager delivers her verdict (with the same price validation as the daily call), and the result is displayed in the same call-panel format — execution strip, choice badge, research report, expert verdict and chart. Every call panel opens with that idea's exact trade execution details at the top — entry decision, position size, risk/reward (target, stop, max loss, reward:risk) and confidence. Prices are validated end to end: every idea must carry a freshly verified as-of price (the desk is forbidden from quoting prices from analyst notes, price targets or older research as current), the manager re-verifies each price before deciding, and a built-in price check flags any idea that ships without a verified quote, whose as-of price is dated, or whose target or stop sits on the wrong side of the live price — flagged prices show an amber warning in the call panel and the production log. The Showrunner then turns the meeting into a playable episode, with the MANAGER speaking in her own distinct voice. When your Experts roster holds an expert whose domain fits the day's board, an interactive guest joins the show: the pipeline picks the best-matched roster expert, and in the show's outlook the EXPERT weighs the manager's call in first person — stating their position (agree, disagree, or agree with reservations) with dated, proven reasons drawn from their recorded knowledge and fresh searches, pushing back on the weakest link, and putting one sharp question to the desk, which the manager answers live. The Analysts board shows their verdict as a point right beside her call — AGREE, DISAGREE or AGREE WITH RESERVATIONS, with their stated reason. The guest speaks in their own distinct voice — pick it under Expert guest voice in Settings (Studio voices), or it auto-pairs; with browser voices it gets its own pitch. The Investment Manager keeps asking the CFA desk for more ideas until her book holds 5 recommendations at 80%+ confidence in each of the four daily-scan categories — STOCKS, PREDICTIONS, BONDS, OTHER — so expect several desk rounds before the show is written. Her re-asks are capped by a desk clock, a time limit you can configure in the Settings tab (default 5 minutes): when the clock runs out she produces the show with whatever high-conviction ideas the desk has delivered so far, and the log keeps her running tally per category. Stock ideas show a live daily candle chart (with technicals) sourced from Finviz beside the desk write-up — press a tile to see it, and pressing the chart opens its live data-source page in a new tab (the Finviz quote page for stocks, the FRED series page for bond yields). Every category has its own contextual data panel: bond ideas map to the matching yield series on FRED (the St. Louis Fed) — treasuries, TIPS real yields, high-yield credit spreads; prediction-market ideas link to the live contract on Polymarket and Kalshi; sports and odds ideas link to current lines across books. If an image is blocked (e.g. in an embedded preview), the panel links straight to the source. In the Episode tab, the daily call displays as a live indicator board instead of the raw script: one minimal card per idea, grouped by the four categories, showing the quantitative indicators (entry decision, position size, risk/reward, confidence) and the qualitative ones (the desk's case, the researchers' best for and against arguments). As the manager reads the script and discusses each opportunity, that idea's card lights up and the indicators she is talking about highlight; toggle back to the raw script with the Show script button — the script is always saved with the episode. From research to verdict, the desk agents apply an advanced analytics toolkit: separating prediction from causation, Double/Debiased-ML-style confounder reasoning, causal-forest thinking about which sub-groups an effect concentrates in, agent-based simulation of emergent system behavior (bank runs, market failures, supply-chain disruptions beyond DSGE-style equilibrium), market-design analysis for pricing claims, and data-harmonization checks on messy or digitized series.

        The Monitor tab

        The 📊 Monitor tab tracks everything your latest episode claimed. Pick an entry from the watchlist dropdown — the trading opportunities from the analyst board (grouped into their trading categories: STOCKS / PREDICTIONS / BONDS / OTHER) alongside Topics, one entry per episode segment listed by its whole topic name; re-checked entries carry a status marker (✓ / ↻ / ✖). The right panel shows, top to bottom: the verdict or point (the call with its action badge and thesis tag, or the segment's point), the quantitative proof (entry, size, risk/reward, confidence — or the dated sources behind a topic), the qualitative proof (the case, for/against arguments, or the segment script), and a still-valid check. Press Re-check with live search and the Monitor Analyst agent searches the live web for what has changed since the episode was produced, then rules the item STILL VALID, SHOULD CHANGE, or EVOLVED — with a severity grade on the change impact: HIGH means the direction flipped (a buy is now a sell — requires immediate attention), MEDIUM means the call stands but entry, size or timing must change, LOW means minor nuance. The Monitor Analyst weighs the evidence with the same advanced analytics toolkit as the desk — causal vs. predictive claims, heterogeneous effects, emergent-behavior simulation, and data-quality checks — before ruling. Re-checks are saved with the episode, and any sources the Monitor Analyst finds are added to the Sources & Dates registry. Producing a new episode starts a fresh watchlist.

        Cloud sync & track record

        The Settings tab has a ☁️ Cloud sync card: sign in with Google and every episode you produce — podcast or analyst call — is saved to your Firebase cloud library, together with one trackable document per board opportunity and per episode topic. The Monitor tab doubles as your track record: each re-check appends to that item's cloud history, and the 🏆 Track record row lets you resolve any opportunity as Won, Lost or Void — or reopen it — with the outcome saved with the episode and synced to the cloud. Press Load cloud library in Settings to bring any past episode back onto this device with its full watchlist and track record intact. Everything works offline too: without sign-in (or without a connection) the app runs entirely on this browser's local storage.

        🌀 Entropic second opinion

        Every BUY / SELL opportunity the desk produces is auto-scored by an embedded causal-entropic engine (F = T·∇Sτ, after Alex Wissner-Gross's work on intelligence as freedom-seeking): it simulates thousands of ten-day futures from the idea's own board numbers — conviction, reward:risk, position size — and asks whether committing to the trade preserves more of the desk's future freedom of action than staying in cash. The board tiles, indicator cards and Monitor panels show the resulting 🌀 F chip (green = the trade keeps more future options open, red = it forecloses them), and every opportunity carries a 🌀 Entropic deep-dive — its regime priors, expected value, ∇Sτ and impairment probability, with editable priors you can re-score against your own view. PREDICTIONS and OTHER ideas are proxy-scored as price paths, so treat those F values as rougher. Treat the score as a second opinion beside the desk's expected value, never a replacement for analyst judgment: walk-forward backtests of this engine show entropic scores can be anti-predictive on some structures — which is exactly why the app surfaces the raw F instead of hiding it. When cloud sync is on, each opportunity's entropic score is stored in your cloud track record alongside its outcome, so over time you can see whether high-F calls really did keep more options open.

        1 · The agents

        Each style has its own agent team — the agent cards above the log show exactly who's working on your episode. While a pipeline is creating an episode or an analyst call, a centered animated thinking icon marks the wait — it pulses in the middle of the screen until the item is created, then disappears. The app stays fully usable in the meantime, so you can still press Stop to cancel the run.
        • 📜 Historian — searches the web for origins, key milestones and dated events in the topic's past.
        • 📡 News Analyst — searches for the latest developments (last ~12 months), each stamped with its date.
        • 🔍 Fact Validator — cross-checks both dossiers with fresh searches, resolves date conflicts, flags unconfirmed claims, and builds one validated timeline from history to today.
        • 🎙️ Showrunner — writes the two-host script to your chosen duration, citing sources and speaking dates naturally.

        2 · Duration

        • Short ≈3 min (about 500 words) — a crisp briefing.
        • Medium ≈7 min (about 1,050 words) — history plus current status.
        • Long ≈13 min (about 1,950 words) — deep dive with outlook.

        3 · Audio & voices

        Pick a voice engine in the Episode tab:

        • 🎙️ Studio voices (recommended) — natural, human-quality speech generated by Mistral Voxtral with your API key. Choose the voice model: Voxtral TTS 3 (voxtral-mini-tts-3-0) is the latest and most natural generation; Voxtral TTS 2 (voxtral-mini-tts-2603) is the previous one. With Voxtral 3 you can also set expressiveness (1.5 calm → 4.5 animated) to tune how lively the delivery sounds. Two distinct preset voices (auto-paired male/female when available) read the episode line-by-line, keeping all the pacing pauses. Audio is cached, so replays are instant; if the API can't be reached, playback automatically falls back to browser voices. Debate episodes get a third voice slot for the CON debater (auto-picked as another distinct studio voice); in browser mode the CON debater uses the host voice pitched deeper, so all three debaters sound different.
        • 🌐 Browser voices — instant and offline, using your system's speech synthesis. The app prefers the smoothest neural/remote voices available, expands symbols and abbreviations for natural reading, and auto-pairs a male and a female voice for the two hosts (dropdowns are gender-labelled; pitch is subtly nudged when only same-gender voices exist).

        Follow along as each spoken line is highlighted; estimated runtime assumes ~155 words per minute.

        Pacing & natural pauses

        The narrator delivers the script sentence by sentence, with real breathing room: full stops get ~0.4 s, questions and exclamations land ~0.5 s, ellipses hold ~0.7 s, and speaker turns pause ~0.8 s so hosts don't talk over each other. A standalone “…” beat line (shown as “· · ·” in the script) becomes a ~1 s dramatic pause after a big point, and every section break rests ~1.5 s before the story continues. Em-dashes become mid-sentence beats, so points land the way a human host would make them.

        4 · Sources & dates

        The Sources & Dates tab lists every source with its URL, coverage date, the agent that found it, and the segments citing it, plus the validator's merged timeline. Nothing in the script goes uncited.

        5 · API key & privacy

        Research calls go to the Mistral Conversations API with the built-in web_search tool, straight from your browser. Your key is kept in local storage on this device and is sent only to api.mistral.ai. No other server is involved.
